When interpreting the luck that arises from our choices, we achieve healthier results by considering the relationships in our lives as a whole rather than taking an egocentric approach. Let me try to explain what our “luck” is actually showing us by looking at one of the most frequently played games: UNO.

> UNO! is a card game played at a table by at least two players. The goal is to play all the cards in your hand before anyone else. The game has a total of 112 cards in four different colors (yellow, blue, green, and red). Each color includes cards numbered 0 through 9. In addition, there are five types of cards without numbers. When a player has no cards left, the round ends, and that player wins. For more information about the game, see [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uno_(card_game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uno_(card_game)).

While playing, each player reflects their own set of meanings (their life experience) in their choices. Some make choices carefully; others choose without much thought. As you play UNO, you can try assigning meanings from your life to each color and choosing accordingly. When your intention behind each choice is positive, your luck increases. Every choice made with negative intentions reduces your luck in the game.

If, at any point in the game, you realize (or sense) that the player next to you has been reminded of a trauma associated with the color green, and you have to choose between two colors, your luck will improve if you choose a color other than green. Seeing the other players not as “enemies” but as “competitors” will likewise contribute to a more positive experience.

If you know that another player is thirsty, choosing green instead of blue so that blue does not remind them of water can be an act of goodwill. Your intentions may be shaped by your own knowledge or life experience, and your set of meanings may not overlap with the other person's. What matters is whether your intention is positive or negative. By paying attention to the other players' choices and using what you know about them to guide your intentions positively, you can increase your chances of winning with the cards you draw.

A player who wins a round of UNO is the one who had the best intentions in that round. The person with the highest score at the end of the game is the best person in that game. Since nothing in nature is random, you should remember what a good person you are when your luck is favorable. Being aware of the divine power that determines which card you choose can make your journey more beautiful. If you lose one or more rounds, continue to maintain your good intentions.
