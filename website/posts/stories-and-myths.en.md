Within our search for meaning in the Universe, different cultures have developed their own ways of expressing themselves over the years. Wherever we are on Earth, we have [read](/en/blog/reading-habit) similar stories featuring different characters. The characteristic features of each region have brought creativity to life through different combinations of colors in each culture.

As we interpreted the laws of nature, we observed that the strong destroyed the weak. The inspiration revealed by The God regarding inequality of opportunity was repeatedly interpreted as individual success. Over time, the concept of power ceased to be personal and became associated with a single god or divine force.

Because the injustices people experienced did not end, divine supplements that emerged within societies continued to shape the decisions people made. We had the opportunity to see people's vulnerability in the face of illness, changing weather, and natural disasters. But those who received divine supplements did not see themselves as reflections of The God on Earth; instead, they became convinced that they were more important than other living beings.

Figures considered more valuable in mythologies were turned into characters that made it harder for societies to see The God. Leaders who took responsibility and used the creativity granted to them to develop their societies defined themselves as rule-makers. Naturally, they treated their divine supplements, which were meant to inspire, carelessly by trying to control the lives of other beings. Every form of control paved the way for the next leader, and cycles of repeated mistakes continued in similar stories.

When the existence of The God was described in stories and mythologies that spread across society, the nature of that existence was not explained in detail, and mystical features were interpreted through invented figures. Destiny, the existence of luck, and divine interventions were described, but their causes were not examined closely enough. Because the characters in the stories were portrayed as more valuable than society as a whole, readers could not see the details in their own lives. Thus, the responsibility entrusted to society as a whole remained with its leaders.

**Let's examine the overlooked details and unanswered questions through an example story:**

### Al-Khiḍr and the wolves

One day, al-Khiḍr withdraws to a dark cave on a deserted mountain. When night falls, the cave is surrounded by howling wolves. Shortly afterward, hungry, wild-looking wolves arrive at the cave's entrance. In this situation, which would normally be deadly for a person, al-Khiḍr does not show the slightest fear; he sits silently and continues to contemplate.

**Questions that should be asked, and answers that explain how every being has the same chance:**

1. Why did al-Khiḍr withdraw to a deserted cave?

Having performed many helpful acts in society, he wanted to be alone to rest and reflect because everyone under stress consulted al-Khiḍr or asked for his help, directly or indirectly.

2. Why did the wolves find that cave?

It was a divine supplement that allowed him to encounter the feelings of other animals, both as a warning (lesson) and as a reward. It was a coincidence that illustrated the relationship between him and the people around him.\
*Without knowing the details, it is difficult to guess what behaviors the wolves reflected in that era and culture. It was simply a chain of life experiences that only al-Khiḍr could see (read).*

3. Why did the wolves not attack al-Khiḍr?

Because al-Khiḍr did not attack or harm any living being, nature's destructive justice posed no threat to him.

4. Why did The God show value to al-Khiḍr?

Sharing and helping others strengthened al-Khiḍr's feelings. The empathy he developed by opening himself (sharing) to other beings in nature opened the door to divine supplements even wider.

5. Why did al-Khiḍr not explain the divine value given to him to society?

If al-Khiḍr had explained why he was lucky, the value he received might have diminished, and he could have been harmed. This was because everyone could solve their own problems independently.

> If the cause of al-Khiḍr's death were known, it would be easier to see what mistake he made and which rules of nature he did not follow.
